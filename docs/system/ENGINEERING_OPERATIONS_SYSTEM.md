# Engineering Operations System

## Engineering domains

The system provides a common orchestration model for:

- Civil and structural engineering
- Mechanical engineering
- Electrical and electronics engineering
- Control systems
- Chemical engineering
- Materials engineering
- Manufacturing and industrial engineering
- Software and computer engineering
- Systems and network engineering
- Robotics
- Automotive engineering
- Aerospace engineering
- Energy engineering
- Environmental engineering
- Biomedical engineering
- Telecommunications
- Marine engineering
- Nuclear research

## Engineering lifecycle

REQUIREMENTS
→ RESEARCH
→ MODEL
→ CALCULATE
→ DESIGN
→ SIMULATE
→ OPTIMIZE
→ PROTOTYPE
→ TEST
→ VERIFY
→ VALIDATE
→ DOCUMENT
→ MONITOR
→ MAINTAIN
→ LEARN

## Evidence hierarchy

The system must distinguish:

- applicable standards
- specifications
- measured data
- engineering calculations
- simulation results
- physical test results
- research findings
- model-generated results
- assumptions
- uncertainties

## Safety boundary

Safety-critical engineering decisions require appropriate standards,
validated engineering software, qualified professionals, independent review,
testing and regulatory approval where applicable.

The system must not treat a generated design or simulation as automatically
safe for real-world deployment.
